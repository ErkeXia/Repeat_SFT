To solve this related rates problem, we consider the ladder, the wall, and the ground forming a right triangle. The ladder is the hypotenuse, always 10 meters in length. Let:

- $ x(t) $ be the horizontal distance from the bottom of the ladder to the wall (increasing at $ \frac{dx}{dt} = 1 \, \text{m/s} $),
- $ y(t) $ be the vertical distance from the top of the ladder to the ground (decreasing, so $ \frac{dy}{dt} < 0 $),
- The ladder length is constant: $ x^2 + y^2 = 100 $.

---

### Step 1: Differentiate the equation

Differentiate both sides of the Pythagorean identity with respect to time $ t $:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(x^2 + y^2) = \frac{d}{dt}(100)
$$
$$
2x \frac{dx}{dt} + 2y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0
$$

Divide both sides by 2:

$$
x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0
$$

Solve for $ \frac{dy}{dt} $:

$$
\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y} \frac{dx}{dt}
$$

---

### Step 2: Plug in known values

We are given:

- $ x = 6 \, \text{m} $,
- $ \frac{dx}{dt} = 1 \, \text{m/s} $,
- The ladder length is 10 m.

Use the Pythagorean theorem to find $ y $:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = 100 \Rightarrow 6^2 + y^2 = 100 \Rightarrow y^2 = 64 \Rightarrow y = 8 \, \text{m}
$$

Now substitute into the expression for $ \frac{dy}{dt} $:

$$
\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{6}{8} \cdot 1 = -\frac{3}{4} \, \text{m/s}
$$

---

### Step 3: Interpret the result

The negative sign indicates that the top of the ladder is **moving downward**, as expected. The question asks:

> "How fast is the top of the ladder sliding down the wall?"

Since the direction (downward) is already specified in the question, and the magnitude is all that is needed, we report the **speed**, not the signed rate.
