To compute the sum of all fractions of the form $\frac{p}{q}$, where $1 \leq p < q \leq n$ and $n \geq 3$, we proceed in a structured and mathematical way.

---

### **Understanding the Problem**

We are summing over all possible fractions $\frac{p}{q}$ such that:

- $p$ and $q$ are natural numbers,
- $p < q$,
- $1 \leq p, q \leq n$.

This includes all such fractions even if they are reducible, as long as $p < q$.

---

### **Step 1: Express the Total Sum**

We can write the total sum $S(n)$ as:

$$
S(n) = \sum_{\substack{1 \leq p < q \leq n}} \frac{p}{q}
$$

To evaluate this sum, we can **fix the denominator $q$** and sum over all valid numerators $p$ such that $1 \leq p < q$. This gives:

$$
S(n) = \sum_{q=2}^{n} \sum_{p=1}^{q-1} \frac{p}{q}
$$

---

### **Step 2: Simplify the Inner Sum**

The inner sum is:

$$
\sum_{p=1}^{q-1} \frac{p}{q} = \frac{1}{q} \sum_{p=1}^{q-1} p = \frac{1}{q} \cdot \frac{(q-1)q}{2} = \frac{q - 1}{2}
$$

Thus, the total sum becomes:

$$
S(n) = \sum_{q=2}^{n} \frac{q - 1}{2}
$$

---

### **Step 3: Evaluate the Final Sum**

Now we compute:

$$
S(n) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{q=2}^{n} (q - 1) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{(n-1)n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{4}
$$
