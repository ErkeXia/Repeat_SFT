### Step 1: Understand the Graph Structure

A convex pentagon has five vertices: $ A, B, C, D, E $. Each vertex is connected to **two adjacent vertices** (forming the **sides**) and to **two non-adjacent vertices** (forming the **diagonals**). This results in:

- **5 sides**: $ AB, BC, CD, DE, EA $
- **5 diagonals**: $ AC, BD, CE, DA, EB $

Each **vertex** is thus connected to **4 other vertices**, meaning each vertex has **degree 4**.

The total number of edges is:

$$
\text{Total edges} = 5 \text{ sides} + 5 \text{ diagonals} = 10
$$

This is exactly the number of edges in a **complete graph $ K_5 $** (since $ \binom{5}{2} = 10 $), and indeed, the **sides and diagonals of the pentagon form the complete graph $ K_5 $**.

### Step 2: Graph Coloring Objective

We are to **edge-color** the graph such that **no two edges that share a common vertex** have the same color. This is the definition of the **chromatic index** of a graph.

The **chromatic index** $ \chi'(G) $ is the **minimum number of colors** needed to color the edges of a graph $ G $ so that no two adjacent edges (i.e., edges sharing a common vertex) have the same color.

---

### Step 3: Apply Vizing's Theorem

According to **Vizing's Theorem**, for any simple graph $ G $, the chromatic index satisfies:

$$
\chi'(G) = \Delta(G) \quad \text{or} \quad \chi'(G) = \Delta(G) + 1
$$

Where $ \Delta(G) $ is the **maximum degree** of the graph.

In $ K_5 $, each vertex is connected to the other 4 vertices, so:

$$
\Delta(K_5) = 4
$$

Hence, the chromatic index of $ K_5 $ is either 4 or 5.

But for **complete graphs**:

- If $ n $ is **even**, $ K_n $ is **Class 1**, and $ \chi'(K_n) = n - 1 $
- If $ n $ is **odd**, $ K_n $ is **Class 2**, and $ \chi'(K_n) = n $

Since $ n = 5 $ is **odd**, we have:

$$
\chi'(K_5) = 5
$$
