$$
\frac{x^2}{27} + y^2 = 1, \quad z = \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}}, \quad z = 0, \quad \text{with } y \geq 0,
$$

we approach this as a triple integral over the region bounded by these surfaces.

---

### **Step 1: Understanding the Geometry**

The equation  
$$
\frac{x^2}{27} + y^2 = 1
$$  
represents an **ellipse** in the $xy$-plane with semi-axes:
- $a = 3\sqrt{3}$ along the $x$-axis,
- $b = 1$ along the $y$-axis.

Since there's no $z$ in this equation, it describes a **cylindrical surface** extending infinitely in the $z$-direction. However, we are only considering the region where:

- $z$ ranges from $0$ to $y/\sqrt{3}$,
- $y \geq 0$ (i.e., the **upper half** of the ellipse).

Thus, the volume is bounded between the **planes** $z = 0$ and $z = y/\sqrt{3}$, and within the upper half of the elliptical cylinder.

---

### **Step 2: Setting Up the Integral**

We can express the volume as a **double integral** over the region $D$ in the $xy$-plane (the upper half of the ellipse), with the integrand being the height of the region in the $z$-direction.

So the volume is:

$$
V = \iint_D \left( \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, dx \, dy
$$

where $D$ is the region in the $xy$-plane defined by:

$$
\frac{x^2}{27} + y^2 \leq 1, \quad y \geq 0
$$

---

### **Step 3: Cartesian Coordinates Integration**

We solve the ellipse equation for $x$:

$$
x = \pm \sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}
$$

So for each $y \in [0, 1]$, $x$ ranges from $-\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}$ to $\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}$. The integral becomes:

$$
V = \int_0^1 \int_{-\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}}^{\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}} \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}} \, dx \, dy
$$

Since the integrand is independent of $x$, the inner integral is:

$$
\int_{-\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}}^{\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}} dx = 2\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)}
$$

So:

$$
V = \int_0^1 \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot 2\sqrt{27(1 - y^2)} \, dy
$$

Simplify constants:

$$
\sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}, \quad \text{so } 2\sqrt{27} = 2 \cdot 3\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3}
$$

$$
V = \int_0^1 \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot 6\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy = \int_0^1 6y \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy
$$

### **Step 4: Evaluate the Integral**

Let’s compute:

$$
V = \int_0^1 6y \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy
$$

- Let $ u = 1 - y^2 \Rightarrow du = -2y \, dy \Rightarrow -\frac{1}{2} du = y \, dy $
- When $ y = 0 $, $ u = 1 $
- When $ y = 1 $, $ u = 0 $

$$
V = 6 \int_0^1 y \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy = 6 \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{2} \int_1^0 \sqrt{u} \, du \right) = 3 \int_0^1 \sqrt{u} \, du

$$
\int_0^1 \sqrt{u} \, du = \int_0^1 u^{1/2} \, du = \left[ \frac{2}{3} u^{3/2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{2}{3}

$$
V = 3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 2
