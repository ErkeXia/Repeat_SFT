### **Geometric Interpretation**

The expression $ |z - a| $ represents the distance from the complex number $ z $ to the point $ a $ in the complex plane. Therefore, the given condition is:

$$
\text{Distance from } z \text{ to } 1 + \text{Distance from } z \text{ to } 3 + 2i = 2\sqrt{2}
$$

This is the **definition of an ellipse** with foci at $ 1 $ and $ 3 + 2i $, and the sum of the distances being constant. However, we must first verify if the constant is greater than the distance between the foci.

Let’s compute the distance between the points $ 1 $ (which is $ (1, 0) $) and $ 3 + 2i $ (which is $ (3, 2) $):

$$
\text{Distance} = \sqrt{(3 - 1)^2 + (2 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}
$$

This is exactly the same as the constant in the equation. When the sum of distances equals the distance between the foci, the ellipse **degenerates** into the **line segment** joining the two foci.

---

### **Objective: Minimize $ |z| $**

We now need to find the **minimum value of $ |z| $** where $ z $ lies on the line segment from $ 1 $ to $ 3 + 2i $, i.e., from $ (1, 0) $ to $ (3, 2) $.

Geometrically, $ |z| $ is the **distance from the origin** to the point $ z $, so we are looking for the point on the segment that is **closest to the origin**.

### **Parametrize the Line Segment**

Let’s parametrize the line segment from $ (1, 0) $ to $ (3, 2) $:

$$
x(t) = 1 + 2t, \quad y(t) = 0 + 2t, \quad t \in [0, 1]

$$
