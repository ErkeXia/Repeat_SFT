We are given a sequence $ a_1 = 1 $ and for $ n \geq 2 $, $ a_n = \dfrac{n}{n^2 - 1} $. The problem asks us to evaluate the sum:

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k \right)
$$

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Sequence

Let’s define the partial product up to term $ k $ as:

$$
P_k = a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k
$$

We are to compute:

$$
S_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n} P_k
$$

We are told:

- $ a_1 = 1 $
- For $ n \geq 2 $, $ a_n = \dfrac{n}{n^2 - 1} = \dfrac{n}{(n - 1)(n + 1)} $

---

### Step 2: Expressing the Product $ P_k $

We can write:

$$
P_k = a_1 \cdot \prod_{i=2}^{k} a_i = \prod_{i=2}^{k} \dfrac{i}{(i - 1)(i + 1)}
$$

This product simplifies nicely because of the telescoping nature of the terms:

$$
P_k = \dfrac{2 \cdot 3 \cdot \cdots \cdot k}{(1 \cdot 2)(2 \cdot 3)(3 \cdot 4) \cdots ((k - 1)(k + 1))}
$$

Grouping the numerator and denominator:

- Numerator: $ k! $
- Denominator: $ \prod_{i=2}^{k} (i - 1)(i + 1) = \prod_{i=2}^{k} (i - 1) \cdot \prod_{i=2}^{k} (i + 1) = (k - 1)! \cdot \dfrac{(k + 1)!}{2!} $

So:

$$
P_k = \dfrac{k!}{(k - 1)! \cdot \dfrac{(k + 1)!}{2}} = \dfrac{2k}{(k + 1)!}
$$

This expression is valid for all $ k \geq 1 $, including $ k = 1 $, since:

$$
P_1 = a_1 = 1 = \dfrac{2 \cdot 1}{(1 + 1)!} = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1
$$

---

### Step 3: Simplifying the Sum

We now evaluate:

$$
S_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n} P_k = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \dfrac{2k}{(k + 1)!}
$$

Let’s simplify $ \dfrac{2k}{(k + 1)!} $ using factorial identities:

$$
\dfrac{2k}{(k + 1)!} = \dfrac{2k}{(k + 1) \cdot k!} = \dfrac{2}{k!} - \dfrac{2}{(k + 1)!}
$$

This gives us a telescoping series:

$$
S_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( \dfrac{2}{k!} - \dfrac{2}{(k + 1)!} \right)
$$

Let’s expand the first few terms:

$$
\left( \dfrac{2}{1!} - \dfrac{2}{2!} \right) + \left( \dfrac{2}{2!} - \dfrac{2}{3!} \right) + \cdots + \left( \dfrac{2}{n!} - \dfrac{2}{(n + 1)!} \right)
$$

All intermediate terms cancel, leaving:

$$
S_n = 2 \left( \dfrac{1}{1!} - \dfrac{1}{(n + 1)!} \right) = 2 \left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{(n + 1)!} \right)
$$

---

### Final Result

$$
\boxed{2 - \dfrac{2}{(n + 1)!}}
