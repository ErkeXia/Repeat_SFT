The foci are located at $ F_1 = (-c, 0) $ and $ F_2 = (c, 0) $, where $ c^2 = a^2 + b^2 $. The **eccentricity** is defined as:

$$
e = \frac{c}{a}
$$

A line passing through $ F_2 $ intersects the **right branch** of the hyperbola at points $ A $ and $ B $. It is given that triangle $ F_1AB $ is **equilateral**.

---

### Step 2: Use of Symmetry and Geometry

Since triangle $ F_1AB $ is equilateral, all sides are equal:

$$
F_1A = F_1B = AB
$$

From the **definition of a hyperbola**, for any point $ P $ on the hyperbola, the absolute difference in distances to the foci is constant:

$$
|F_1P - F_2P| = 2a
$$

Since both $ A $ and $ B $ lie on the **right branch**, we have:

$$
F_1A - F_2A = 2a \quad \text{and} \quad F_1B - F_2B = 2a
$$

Also, from the equilateral triangle condition, we know that $ F_1A = F_1B = AB $. This implies that points $ A $ and $ B $ are equidistant from $ F_1 $, and the line segment $ AB $ is equal in length to $ F_1A $.

---

### Step 3: Midpoint Condition

Since both $ A $ and $ B $ lie on a line through $ F_2 $, and since they are equidistant from $ F_1 $ and equidistant from $ F_2 $, the midpoint of segment $ AB $ must be $ F_2 $.

Let $ A = (x, y) $, then the coordinates of $ B $ must be:

$$
B = (2c - x, -y)
$$

---

### Step 4: Use of Rotation

Since triangle $ F_1AB $ is equilateral, rotating point $ A $ about $ F_1 $ by 60° should give point $ B $. Using a 60° rotation matrix:

$$
B = R_{60^\circ}(A - F_1) + F_1
$$

This leads to a system of equations for the coordinates of $ B $ in terms of $ x $ and $ y $. Equating this with the midpoint condition gives a system of equations:

$$
\begin{cases}
3x - \sqrt{3}y = 5c \\
\sqrt{3}x + 3y = -\sqrt{3}c
\end{cases}
$$

Solving this system yields:

$$
x = c, \quad y = -\frac{2\sqrt{3}c}{3}
$$

---

### Step 5: Plug into Hyperbola Equation

Substitute the coordinates $ (c, -\frac{2\sqrt{3}c}{3}) $ into the hyperbola equation:

$$
\frac{c^2}{a^2} - \frac{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{3}c}{3}\right)^2}{b^2} = 1
$$

Compute each term:

- $ \frac{c^2}{a^2} = 3 $ (since we will find $ a = \frac{c}{\sqrt{3}} $)
- $ \frac{(4 \cdot 3 c^2 / 9)}{b^2} = \frac{12c^2}{9b^2} = \frac{4c^2}{3b^2} $

Set the equation:

$$
3 - \frac{4c^2}{3b^2} = 1 \Rightarrow \frac{4c^2}{3b^2} = 2 \Rightarrow b^2 = \frac{2c^2}{3}
$$

Now use the identity $ c^2 = a^2 + b^2 $:

$$
c^2 = a^2 + \frac{2c^2}{3} \Rightarrow a^2 = \frac{c^2}{3}
$$

Thus:

$$
a = \frac{c}{\sqrt{3}}, \quad e = \frac{c}{a} = \sqrt{3}
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{3}}
$$
