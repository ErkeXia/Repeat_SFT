$$
A = 100101102103\ldots798799

We are asked to find the **remainder when $ A $ is divided by 126**, i.e., compute $ A \mod 126 $.

---

### Step 1: Prime Factorization of 126

We begin by factoring 126:

$$
126 = 2 \times 7 \times 9
$$

Since these factors are pairwise coprime, we can use the **Chinese Remainder Theorem** (CRT) to compute:

$$
A \mod 126 \iff A \mod 2, \ A \mod 7, \ A \mod 9
$$

---

### Step 2: Compute $ A \mod 2 $

Since $ A $ is a concatenation of three-digit numbers, the **last digit of $ A $** is the last digit of the last number, which is **799**, so the last digit is **9**.

- 9 is **odd**, so $ A \equiv 1 \mod 2 $

### Step 3: Compute $ A \mod 9 $

A number modulo 9 is equal to the **sum of its digits modulo 9**.

We compute the total digit sum of all numbers from 100 to 799.

There are $ 700 $ such numbers (799 - 100 + 1 = 700), each 3 digits long, so:

- **Total digit count**: $ 700 \times 3 = 2100 $ digits
- We compute the sum of all digits across all numbers

Let’s compute the sum of digits in each place separately:

#### Hundreds place:

Each hundreds digit from 1 to 7 appears **100 times** (for numbers 100–199, ..., 700–799):

$$
\text{Sum} = 100 \times (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7) = 100 \times 28 = 2800
$$

#### Tens and Units places:

In each group of 100 numbers, the tens and units digits cycle from 00 to 99. So in each group:

- Each digit from 0 to 9 appears 10 times in the tens place
- Same for the units place

So for each group of 100 numbers:

$$
\text{Sum of tens and units digits} = 10 \times (0 + 1 + 2 + \ldots + 9) \times 2 = 10 \times 45 \times 2 = 900
$$

With 7 such groups:

$$
\text{Total tens and units sum} = 7 \times 900 = 6300
$$

#### Total digit sum:

$$
2800 + 6300 = 9100
$$

So:

$$
A \equiv 9100 \mod 9
$$

Now compute $ 9100 \mod 9 $:

$$
9100 \equiv (9 + 1 + 0 + 0) = 10 \equiv 1 \mod 9
$$

### Step 4: Compute $ A \mod 7 $

To compute this, we observe that $ A $ is formed by **concatenating numbers** from 100 to 799, and each step involves multiplying the current number by 1000 (to shift it left by three digits) and adding the next number.

Let’s define a recurrence:

$$
\text{Let } c_k = (c_{k-1} \times 1000 + n_k) \mod 7
$$

Since $ 1000 \equiv -1 \mod 7 $, this simplifies to:

$$
c_k \equiv -c_{k-1} + n_k \mod 7
$$

Unfolding the recurrence gives:

$$
c_k \equiv (-1)^k n_1 + (-1)^{k-1} n_2 + \ldots + (-1)^1 n_k \mod 7

Or:

$$
c_k \equiv \sum_{i=1}^{k} (-1)^{k - i} n_i \mod 7

A \equiv 1 \mod 2 \\

x \equiv 1 \mod 2 \\
x \equiv 0 \mod 7 \\
