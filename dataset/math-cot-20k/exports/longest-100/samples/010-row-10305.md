To solve the problem, we are given a function $ g(k) $, which returns the **greatest odd divisor** of a natural number $ k $. We are to compute the sum:

$$
g(1) + g(2) + g(3) + \cdots + g(2^n)
$$

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Function $ g(k) $

- For **odd** numbers $ k $, the greatest odd divisor is simply $ k $ itself.
- For **even** numbers $ k $, the greatest odd divisor is obtained by repeatedly dividing $ k $ by 2 until it becomes odd.

For example:
- $ g(3) = 3 $, $ g(20) = 5 $, $ g(12) = 3 $, $ g(16) = 1 $, etc.

---

### Step 2: Observing the Structure of the Sum

Let’s denote the total sum as:

$$
S(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{2^n} g(k)
$$

We can divide the numbers from 1 to $ 2^n $ into **odd** and **even**:

- **Odd numbers** from 1 to $ 2^n $: There are $ 2^{n-1} $ such numbers. The sum of all odd numbers from 1 to $ 2^n $ is:

$$
\text{Sum of odds} = 1 + 3 + 5 + \cdots + (2^n - 1)
$$

This is an arithmetic sequence with $ 2^{n-1} $ terms, first term 1, and last term $ 2^n - 1 $. The average is:

$$
\text{Average} = \frac{1 + (2^n - 1)}{2} = 2^{n-1}
$$

So the sum is:

$$
\text{Sum of odds} = 2^{n-1} \cdot 2^{n-1} = 4^{n-1}
$$

- **Even numbers** from 1 to $ 2^n $: These are $ 2, 4, 6, \ldots, 2^n $. Each even number $ k $ can be written as $ 2 \cdot m $, where $ m $ is an integer from 1 to $ 2^{n-1} $. The greatest odd divisor of $ 2 \cdot m $ is $ g(m) $, since dividing out the factor of 2 doesn’t change the odd part.

So the sum over even numbers is:

$$
\sum_{\substack{k=1 \\ k \text{ even}}}^{2^n} g(k) = \sum_{m=1}^{2^{n-1}} g(m) = S(n-1)
$$

### Step 3: Deriving the Recurrence

Combining the two parts, we get:

$$
S(n) = \text{Sum of odds} + \text{Sum over evens} = 4^{n-1} + S(n-1)
$$

This is a recurrence relation with base case:

$$
S(1) = g(1) + g(2) = 1 + 1 = 2
$$

---

### Step 4: Solving the Recurrence

We now solve the recurrence:

$$
S(n) = 4^{n-1} + S(n-1), \quad S(1) = 2
$$

Unrolling the recurrence:

$$
S(n) = 4^{n-1} + 4^{n-2} + \cdots + 4^1 + S(1)
$$

This is a geometric series:

$$
S(n) = 2 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} 4^k
$$

The sum of a geometric series $ \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} 4^k $ is:

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} 4^k = \frac{4(4^{n-1} - 1)}{4 - 1} = \frac{4^n - 4}{3}
$$

So:

$$
S(n) = 2 + \frac{4^n - 4}{3} = \frac{4^n + 2}{3}
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\dfrac{4^n + 2}{3}}
$$
