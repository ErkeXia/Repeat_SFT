### Step 1: Express the Vectors in Terms of Known Vectors

We begin by expressing the diagonals $\vec{AC}$ and $\vec{BD}$ in terms of the side vectors:

- $\vec{AC} = \vec{AB} + \vec{BC}$
- $\vec{BD} = \vec{BC} + \vec{CD}$

$$
\vec{AC} \cdot \vec{BD} = (\vec{AB} + \vec{BC}) \cdot (\vec{BC} + \vec{CD})
$$

Expanding the dot product:

$$
\vec{AC} \cdot \vec{BD} = \vec{AB} \cdot \vec{BC} + \vec{AB} \cdot \vec{CD} + \vec{BC} \cdot \vec{BC} + \vec{BC} \cdot \vec{CD}
$$

### Step 2: Use Vector Identities

We apply vector identities to express each term in terms of the magnitudes of the vectors and their dot products.

- $|\vec{AC}|^2 = |\vec{AB}|^2 + |\vec{BC}|^2 + 2\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{BC}$
- $|\vec{BD}|^2 = |\vec{BC}|^2 + |\vec{CD}|^2 + 2\vec{BC} \cdot \vec{CD}$

From these, we can solve for the dot products:

- $\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{BC} = \frac{|\vec{AC}|^2 - |\vec{AB}|^2 - |\vec{BC}|^2}{2}$
- $\vec{BC} \cdot \vec{CD} = \frac{|\vec{BD}|^2 - |\vec{BC}|^2 - |\vec{CD}|^2}{2}$

$$
\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{CD} = \vec{AB} \cdot (\vec{AD} - \vec{AB} - \vec{BC})
$$

This simplifies to:

$$
\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{CD} = \vec{AB} \cdot \vec{AD} - |\vec{AB}|^2 - \vec{AB} \cdot \vec{BC}
$$

Using the law of cosines in triangle $ABD$:

$$
\vec{AB} \cdot \vec{AD} = \frac{|\vec{AB}|^2 + |\vec{AD}|^2 - |\vec{BD}|^2}{2}
$$

Substituting all expressions into the expanded dot product, and after careful algebraic simplification, we derive the final formula:

$$
\vec{AC} \cdot \vec{BD} = \frac{|\vec{AD}|^2 - |\vec{AB}|^2 + |\vec{BC}|^2 - |\vec{CD}|^2}{2}
$$
