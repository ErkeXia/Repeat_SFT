- Each cell contains an integer from the set {0, 1, 2, 3, 4}.

- We must count how many such squares exist.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Constraints

A 3×3 square has:

- 3 rows  
- 3 columns  
- 2 diagonals  

That gives a total of **8 lines**, and each of these **must sum to 4**.

Let’s label the square as follows:

```
a b c  
d e f  
g h i
```

We are to assign values from {0,1,2,3,4} to the 9 cells such that:

- a + b + c = 4  
- d + e + f = 4  
- g + h + i = 4  
- a + d + g = 4  
- b + e + h = 4  
- c + f + i = 4  
- a + e + i = 4  
- c + e + g = 4

---

### Step 2: Total Sum of All Cells

Each line sums to 4, and there are 8 lines, so the **total sum of all cell values across all lines** is:

$$
8 \times 4 = 32
$$

However, each cell appears in multiple lines:

- **Corner cells** (a, c, g, i): appear in **3 lines**
- **Edge cells** (b, d, f, h): appear in **2 lines**
- **Center cell** (e): appears in **4 lines**

Let’s define:

- $ C = a + c + g + i $ (sum of corners)
- $ E = b + d + f + h $ (sum of edges)
- $ e $ = center cell

Then the total sum of all lines is:

$$
3C + 2E + 4e = 32
$$

Also, the **total sum of all cells** is:

$$
C + E + e = 12
$$

We now solve this system of equations:

1. $ 3C + 2E + 4e = 32 $  
2. $ C + E + e = 12 $

### Step 3: Solving the System

From the second equation:

$$
C = 12 - E - e
$$

Substitute into the first equation:

$$
3(12 - E - e) + 2E + 4e = 32  
36 - 3E - 3e + 2E + 4e = 32  
36 - E + e = 32  
\Rightarrow E = 4 + e
$$

Now substitute back to find $ C $:

$$
C = 12 - (4 + e) - e = 8 - 2e
$$

So we now have:

- $ C = 8 - 2e $
- $ E = 4 + e $

Now test possible integer values of $ e \in \{0,1,2,3,4\} $:

| e | C = 8 - 2e | E = 4 + e | Feasibility of C and E (each cell ≤4)? |
