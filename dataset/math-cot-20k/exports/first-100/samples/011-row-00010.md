1. The sequence $ \{a_n\} $ has the property that the sum of the first $ n $ terms is

   S_n = 2a_n - 1

   $$
   b_1 = 3, \quad b_{k+1} = b_k + a_k \quad \text{for } k \geq 1.
   $$

---

### Step 1: Determine an explicit formula for $ a_n $

We are given:
$$
S_n = a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n = 2a_n - 1
$$
Also, using the recursive nature of sums:
$$
S_n = S_{n-1} + a_n
$$
Substitute both expressions for $ S_n $:
$$
2a_n - 1 = (2a_{n-1} - 1) + a_n
$$
Simplify:
$$
2a_n - 1 = 2a_{n-1} - 1 + a_n \Rightarrow a_n = 2a_{n-1}
$$
This is a geometric recurrence relation with a common ratio of 2.

To find the initial term $ a_1 $, use the formula:
$$
S_1 = 2a_1 - 1 \Rightarrow a_1 = 2a_1 - 1 \Rightarrow a_1 = 1
$$
So the sequence $ a_n $ is:
$$
a_n = 2^{n-1}
$$

---

### Step 2: Derive a formula for $ b_n $

We are given:
$$
b_{k+1} = b_k + a_k, \quad b_1 = 3
$$
This is a recurrence relation where each term is the previous term plus $ a_k = 2^{k-1} $. Expanding a few terms:
- $ b_2 = b_1 + a_1 = 3 + 1 = 4 $
- $ b_3 = b_2 + a_2 = 4 + 2 = 6 $
- $ b_4 = b_3 + a_3 = 6 + 4 = 10 $
- $ b_5 = b_4 + a_4 = 10 + 8 = 18 $

We can generalize this:
$$
b_n = b_1 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} a_k = 3 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} 2^{k-1}
$$
The sum is a geometric series:
$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} 2^{k-1} = 2^{n-1} - 1
$$
So:
$$
b_n = 3 + (2^{n-1} - 1) = 2^{n-1} + 2
$$

---

### Step 3: Compute the sum $ T_n = b_1 + b_2 + \cdots + b_n $

Now that we have:
$$
b_n = 2^{n-1} + 2
$$

$$
T_n = \sum_{k=1}^n b_k = \sum_{k=1}^n (2^{k-1} + 2) = \sum_{k=1}^n 2^{k-1} + \sum_{k=1}^n 2
$$

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n 2^{k-1} = 2^n - 1
$$

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n 2 = 2n
$$
Therefore:
$$
T_n = (2^n - 1) + 2n = 2^n + 2n - 1
