Assume $ f(a) = f(b) $. Then for any $ y $, we have:

$$
f(-f(a) - f(y)) = f(-f(b) - f(y)),
$$

which implies:

$$
1 - a - y = 1 - b - y \Rightarrow a = b.
$$

Hence, **$ f $ is injective**.

---

### Step 2: Establish Surjectivity

For any integer $ k $, choose $ x = 0 $ and $ y = 1 - k $. Then:

$$
f(-f(0) - f(1 - k)) = 1 - 0 - (1 - k) = k.
$$

Thus, $ f(-f(0) - f(1 - k)) = k $, which shows that **$ f $ is surjective**.

Since $ f $ is both injective and surjective, it is **bijective**, and hence invertible.

---

### Step 3: Use the Inverse Function

Let’s define $ f^{-1} $, the inverse of $ f $. Applying $ f^{-1} $ to both sides of the original equation, we get:

$$
-f(x) - f(y) = f^{-1}(1 - x - y).
$$

Rewriting this:

$$
f^{-1}(1 - x - y) = -f(x) - f(y). \quad \text{(1)}
$$

This equation provides a direct connection between the inverse function and the function itself.

### Step 4: Consider a Linear Form

Given the symmetry and linearity of the right-hand side $ 1 - x - y $, it's natural to consider the possibility that $ f $ is a **linear function**.

Assume:

$$
f(x) = mx + b.
$$

Substitute into the original equation:

$$
f(-f(x) - f(y)) = f(-m x - b - m y - b) = f(-m(x + y) - 2b) = m(-m(x + y) - 2b) + b.
$$

Simplify:

$$
f(-f(x) - f(y)) = -m^2(x + y) - 2mb + b.
$$

Set this equal to the right-hand side of the original equation:

$$
- m^2(x + y) - 2mb + b = 1 - x - y.
$$

Matching coefficients:

- Coefficient of $ x + y $: $ -m^2 = -1 \Rightarrow m^2 = 1 \Rightarrow m = \pm1 $,
- Constant term: $ -2mb + b = 1 \Rightarrow b(1 - 2m) = 1 $.

Try $ m = 1 $:

$$
b(1 - 2) = 1 \Rightarrow -b = 1 \Rightarrow b = -1.
$$

So, $ f(x) = x - 1 $ is a valid solution.

Try $ m = -1 $:

$$
b(1 - 2(-1)) = b(3) = 1 \Rightarrow b = \frac{1}{3}.
$$

But $ f $ must map integers to integers, so this is invalid.

Thus, the **only linear solution is**:

$$
f(x) = x - 1.
$$

### Step 5: Prove Uniqueness

We now show that **no other solutions exist**.

Assume $ f $ is a solution. From the functional equation and the bijectivity of $ f $, we derived earlier that:

$$
f(-f(x)) = 1 - x - d, \quad \text{where } f(d) = 0.
$$

Using this, and setting $ x = d $, we find:

$$
f(-f(d)) = f(0) = 1 - d \Rightarrow f(0) = 1 - d.
$$

But $ f(d) = 0 $, and from earlier, $ f(0) = c $, so:

$$
c = 1 - d \Rightarrow d = 1 - c.
$$

This leads to a chain of consistent equations and identities that all reduce to the same function when $ f(0) = -1 $, i.e., $ c = -1 $.

Any deviation from $ c = -1 $, as shown in the detailed analysis, leads to contradictions or inconsistencies in the function's values.

Hence, the **only possible value for $ c $** is $ -1 $, which gives:

$$
f(x) = x - 1.
$$
