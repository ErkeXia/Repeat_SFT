---

### Key Observations

- **Natural numbers** mean all $ x_i \in \mathbb{Z}^+ $.
- The equation is a balance between **sum** and **product** of $ n $ numbers.
- The product grows **much faster** than the sum for large values, especially when most $ x_i = 1 $, so **1s** are often used to keep the product small.

---

### Strategy

We aim to **maximize one of the $ x_i $** values while satisfying the equation.

A known and general solution is to take:
- $ n - 2 $ ones,
- one 2,
- and one number $ n $.

Let’s verify this configuration:

- **Sum** = $ 2 + n + (n - 2)\cdot1 = 2 + n + n - 2 = 2n $,
- **Product** = $ 2 \cdot n \cdot 1^{n - 2} = 2n $.

Thus, the sum equals the product, and the maximum value in the tuple is $ \boxed{n} $.

Suppose we try to use a number **greater than $ n $** in the tuple. Then, the product becomes significantly larger than the sum, making it impossible to satisfy the equation.

  - So, $ 2n \neq n+1 $, and the equation fails.

This shows that **no number larger than $ n $** can be used in a solution that satisfies the given equation for any $ n \geq 2 $.
