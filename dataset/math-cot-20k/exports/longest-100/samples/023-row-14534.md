In graph-theoretic terms, this is saying:  
> For every pair of vertices $ u, v $, there exists a vertex $ w $ such that $ w \rightarrow u $ and $ w \rightarrow v $.

This is a **2-covering** condition: every pair of vertices is "covered" by a third vertex that dominates both.

### Key Observations

Let’s denote the number of bodyguards as $ n $. The number of distinct pairs of bodyguards is $ \binom{n}{2} $.

In a tournament, each vertex (bodyguard) has an **out-degree** $ k_i $, representing the number of bodyguards it has defeated. The number of pairs that this vertex covers (i.e., the number of pairs both of which it defeats) is:

$$
\binom{k_i}{2}
$$

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} \binom{k_i}{2}
$$

We want this sum to be **at least** $ \binom{n}{2} $, since every pair must be covered at least once.
