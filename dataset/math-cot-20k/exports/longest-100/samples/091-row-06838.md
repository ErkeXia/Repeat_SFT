> For any subset $ S $ of $ m $ vertices, there exists **exactly one** vertex $ v \notin S $ such that $ v $ is adjacent to **all** vertices in $ S $.

### Step 1: Understand the Implication of the Condition

Let’s denote the total number of passengers (vertices) as $ N $, and the degree of a vertex $ v $ as $ d_v $, which represents the number of friends that person $ v $ has.

Since the condition specifies that each such subset $ S $ has **exactly one** such $ v $, the total number of such pairs is:

$$
\sum_{v \in V} \binom{d_v}{m} = \binom{N}{m}
$$

This is a crucial identity that must be satisfied for the graph to meet the problem’s conditions.

### Step 2: Try a Candidate Graph — Complete Graph

Consider the **complete graph $ K_{m+1} $**, which has $ N = m + 1 $ vertices. In this graph, every vertex is connected to every other vertex. That means:

- Each vertex has degree $ m $.
- Any subset $ S $ of $ m $ vertices will have **exactly one** vertex not in $ S $, which is connected to all of them.

This satisfies the condition: every $ m $-subset has a **unique** common friend (the one not in the subset), and no other vertex is connected to all $ m $ vertices in $ S $, since all vertices are only connected to the other $ m $ vertices.

Let’s verify the identity:

$$
\sum_{v \in V} \binom{d_v}{m} = (m+1) \cdot \binom{m}{m} = m+1
$$
$$
\binom{N}{m} = \binom{m+1}{m} = m+1
$$

So the identity holds. Therefore, the complete graph $ K_{m+1} $ is a valid solution to the problem.

### Step 3: Can There Be Another Graph with Higher Maximum Degree?

Suppose there exists another graph with $ N > m+1 $ vertices that satisfies the condition and has a vertex with degree $ > m $. Then that vertex must be connected to more than $ m $ other vertices.

Let’s denote such a vertex as $ v $, with degree $ d_v = m + 1 $. Then, $ v $ is connected to $ m + 1 $ other vertices. Consider the set $ S $ consisting of any $ m $ of these $ m + 1 $ neighbors of $ v $. Since $ v $ is connected to all of them, it is a common friend of $ S $. But if another vertex is also connected to all $ m $ vertices in $ S $, that would violate the **uniqueness** of the common friend.

Hence, **no other vertex** can be connected to all $ m $ vertices in any subset $ S $, or else the uniqueness condition fails.

This implies that the **maximum possible degree** in such a graph is $ m $, and this is achieved in the complete graph $ K_{m+1} $.

In the valid graph $ K_{m+1} $, **every** vertex has degree $ m $, so the person with the **most** friends has exactly $ m $ friends.
