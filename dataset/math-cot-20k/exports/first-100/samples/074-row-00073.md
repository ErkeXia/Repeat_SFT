### Step 1: Use the Angle Sum in a Quadrilateral

In any convex quadrilateral, the sum of the interior angles is:

$$
\angle A + \angle B + \angle C + \angle D = 360^\circ
$$

Substituting the known value of $ \angle C $:

$$
\angle A + \angle B + \angle D = 360^\circ - 57^\circ = 303^\circ
$$

Thus, if we can determine $ \angle A + \angle B $, we can find $ \angle D $ as:

$$
\angle D = 303^\circ - (\angle A + \angle B)
$$

### Step 2: Use Trigonometric Identities

We are given:

$$
\sin \angle A + \sin \angle B = \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\cos \angle A + \cos \angle B = 2 - \sqrt{2}
$$

Using the sum-to-product identities:

$$
\sin A + \sin B = 2 \sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)
$$
$$
\cos A + \cos B = 2 \cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)

$$

$$
2 \cos\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{y}{2}\right) = 2 - \sqrt{2}
$$

Dividing the first equation by the second:

$$
\frac{\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}{\cos\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2 - \sqrt{2}}

Simplifying:

$$
\tan\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2 - \sqrt{2}}
