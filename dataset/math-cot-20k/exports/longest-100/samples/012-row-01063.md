   - Since $ DE \parallel BC $ and both $ D $ and $ E $ lie on $ AB $ and $ AC $, respectively, and the segment $ DE $ is parallel to $ BC $, the triangle $ \triangle ABC $ must have $ D $ and $ E $ as **midpoints** of $ AB $ and $ AC $, respectively.
   - This is a direct consequence of the **midline theorem**: the segment connecting the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half its length.

2. **Given Lengths Imply Triangle Side Lengths**:
   - Since $ D $ is the midpoint of $ AB $, and $ BD = 2 $, then $ AB = 2 \times BD = 4 $.
   - Similarly, since $ E $ is the midpoint of $ AC $, and $ CE = 1 $, then $ AC = 2 \times CE = 2 $.

3. **Perpendiculars from Midpoint**:
   - The fact that $ MD \perp AB $ and $ ME \perp AC $ suggests that $ M $ lies in a special position relative to triangle $ ABC $, and in combination with the midline $ DE \parallel BC $, this strongly implies that $ ABC $ is a **right triangle at $ A $**.
   - In a right triangle at $ A $, the perpendiculars from the midpoint of the hypotenuse to the legs are **equal in length** to half the length of the opposite leg.

4. **Coordinate Geometry Confirmation**:
   - Place point $ A $ at the origin $ (0, 0) $, point $ B $ at $ (4, 0) $, and point $ C $ at $ (0, 2) $.
   - Then:
     - $ M $, the midpoint of $ BC $, is at $ (2, 1) $,
     - $ D $, the midpoint of $ AB $, is at $ (2, 0) $,
     - $ E $, the midpoint of $ AC $, is at $ (0, 1) $.
   - The segment $ DE $ connects $ (2, 0) $ to $ (0, 1) $, and its slope is $ -\frac{1}{2} $, matching the slope of $ BC $, which connects $ (4, 0) $ to $ (0, 2) $. So $ DE \parallel BC $ is satisfied.
   - The segment $ MD $ connects $ (2, 1) $ to $ (2, 0) $, which is vertical and hence perpendicular to $ AB $ (which lies on the x-axis).
